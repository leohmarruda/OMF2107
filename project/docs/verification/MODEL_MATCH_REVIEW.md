# Jaguar–Shadow mesh match review — 2026-10-14

**Decision:** Jaguar and Shadow's silhouettes were accepted for the prototype
blockout milestone on 2026-10-14. This review records match presentation and
remaining motion work; it is not approval of final surfacing or a complete
animation set.

## What runs in the prototype

The [Jaguar](../../robots/jaguar/blockout/README.md) and
[Shadow](../../robots/shadow/blockout/README.md) editable glTF blockouts share
their source geometry with `project/prototype/model_meshes.h`, generated by
`project/tools/generate_model_meshes.py`. The prototype renders those
triangles and maps each named rigid part to shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip,
knee, and ankle poses. Source OpenOMF state still owns position, facing,
health, hit, block, and move timing. The model transform is presentation only.

The current pose set includes idle sway, walking stride, jump tuck, standing
guard, punch, kick, hit recoil, and one signature pose sequence for each HAR.
Jaguar punch (animation 42), kick (41), and down punch (30), plus Shadow kick
(42) and punch (43), use the existing source-sprite pose catalog. Jaguar's
concussion-cannon animation 21 and Shadow's Shadow Punch animation 23 now
have sprite-keyed arm poses. The [signature move trace](SIGNATURE_MOVE_TRACE.md)
records both at near and distant spacing. The source frames were exported locally and
matched to the corresponding higher-resolution Remastered atlas frames by
pixel fingerprint. The approved OMF2107 three-view sheets define the meshes;
the two game sources guide motion only. Each robot also has a portable
rigid-joint glTF with eight editor preview clips. Those clips are useful for
asset review; source-sprite pose selection in the prototype remains the
match presentation path. Neither glTF is a final skinned deformation rig.

## Shadow close-throw trace — 2026-10-27

`rectests/SHADOW-CLOSE-THROW.REC` is the measurement harness's fixture for Shadow's
close forward-punch throw (`P6`, animation 20) at a 30-unit start gap, saved so it
can be replayed with `replay-rec.ps1 -Rec SHADOW-CLOSE-THROW`. In the trace:

- Shadow plays animation 20 on ticks 126–171 (46 ticks); Jaguar enters throw state 7
  (animation 9) on tick 126.
- Jaguar's health falls from 213 to 152 (61) on tick 147.
- Jaguar is carried from x=164 across Shadow (x=217) to the right wall at x=298, enters
  wall-damage state 15 on tick 153, and is back on his feet around tick 171–177.

This throw moves the opponent about 134 source units, far more than Jaguar's close
throw in the corner fixture. Local captures at ticks 130, 138, 144, and 153 are in
`project/build/mesh-match-ticks/shadow-throw-*.bmp`. At tick 130 the grapple reads, with
both meshes intertwined as the source places them. At ticks 138 and 153 the fight camera
frames the thrown fighter high in the picture and the floor is largely out of view at
tick 153 (Jaguar at the wall, Shadow low at the bottom). I judged this from stills only;
whether it is a camera-follow problem during long throws, or fine at normal speed,
needs a look at the live replay.

Live replay review of Jaguar (the thrown fighter), 2026-10-27, checked against the trace:

- **Instant move between ticks 122 and 123.** This is the fixture, not the game: the
  harness places both fighters at their test start positions at tick 123 (Jaguar x
  66.8 to 190.0, Shadow 248.2 to 220.0). `SHADOW-CLOSE-THROW.REC` therefore contains
  a teleport; watch it from tick 124. A recording without one would need a real
  approach.
- **Fallen pose at 143, jump pose at 144, fallen again at 165.** The source changes
  Jaguar's sprite from 11 to 15 on tick 144 and moves him from (252, 165) to
  (265, 145) in the same tick; he is on sprite 11 again at 165 while falling. The 3D
  view follows those source sprites, but the poses authored for them differ a lot
  (sprite 11 is a tilted-back, dropped pose; sprite 15 is nearly upright). Whether
  that reads as a tumble or as a glitch depends on how the original sprites look.
  Checked against the original `FIGHTR0.AF` frames: sprite 11 is a fall tilted back,
  14 lies flat, 15 is upright with one knee raised, and 16 is upright with an arm
  raised, so the authored poses match the originals and the upright mid-air frame is
  in the source animation. No pose change was made.
- **Facing flips on the ground between ticks 183 and 184.** The source turns Jaguar
  toward Shadow on the tick his animation changes from 9 to 2 (get-up). The viewer
  snapped the mesh through 180 degrees with no turn. The viewer now eases the
  displayed yaw over about 0.15 s (presentation only; captures and large frame jumps
  still snap). This was built and the capture path re-checked, but the eased turn only
  runs in the live viewer and has not been watched yet.
- **Upright "jump" pose while falling between ticks 144 and 164.** This is the source,
  and per OpenOMF's own comment it comes from the original game's data. Ticks 144–147
  show sprite 15 during the throw. When the thrown fighter hits the wall at tick 152,
  `arena_har_hit_wall_hook` (`src/game/scenes/arena.c`) plays animation 9 with the custom
  frame string `hQ1-hQ7-x-3Q5-x-2L5-x-2M900`, commented "from MASTER.DAT". Letters
  index sprites (Q is 16, L is 11, M is 12), so the string holds sprite 16 (upright, arm
  raised) for 1 + 7 + 5 = 13 ticks while he slides down the wall (vy rises from 5.5 to
  8.3), then shows sprite 11 (falling) from tick 165 and sprite 12 on landing. The trace
  matches this exactly (sprite 16 on ticks 152–164, sprite 11 at 165, sprite 12 at 170).
  I have not checked the string against the original `MASTER.DAT` myself, only the
  OpenOMF source comment and the trace.

## Live playtest — 2026-10-27

Full Stadium matches were played live with both the blockout and Meshy models,
and both were judged good overall. Findings:

- **Body overlap during kicks and falls.** The meshes visibly intersect during
  kicks and when a fighter falls. The close-contact separation offset above
  covers only the sampled standing punch and kick fixtures, so these cases are
  open. OpenOMF owns positions, so any fix must stay render-only.
- **Crossing fighters.** Bodies often intersect when the fighters cross each
  other. OpenOMF has no body-to-body collision between HARs:
  `har_collide` only checks attack hit points, `har_check_closeness` just sets
  an AI-only `close` flag, and positions are clamped only to the arena walls
  (`src/game/objects/har.c`). The overlap is therefore faithful to the source;
  the thicker 3D meshes only make it more visible. Any push-away velocity comes
  from move scripts and hit reactions, not continuous collision (not traced).
- **Decision (2026-10-27):** accept the kick, fall, and crossing overlap for
  now. If revisited, the fix must be render-only mesh separation that leaves
  OpenOMF positions and hit results untouched.
- **Close punches not observed.** Punches at close range usually became throws
  in live play, so the close-punch fixture was not exercised. A throw triggers
  when the HAR distance is within the move's range times the `throw_range` match
  setting (default 100%; `har.c`). Throw animations have not been compared with
  the 3D presentation.
