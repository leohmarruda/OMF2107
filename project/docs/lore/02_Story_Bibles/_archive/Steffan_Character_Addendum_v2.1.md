# OMF 2107 — Steffan Thomas characterization addendum v2.1

**Authority:** User-specified characterization (November 2, 2026), replacing weaker “primarily vain celebrity” interpretations without deleting historical documents. **Status:** Core psychological direction established; particular causal readings, romance routes, and screenplay incidents remain author-review proposals.

## Established character traits

1. **Exceptionally intelligent and competent.** Steffan is a formidable analytical, technical and athletic talent. His rivalry with Dante cannot depend on arbitrary stupidity or having been overpraised. He is a real contender who works hard, calculates well, and routinely produces accurate tactical insights.
2. **Emotionally immature, especially under threat to his status.** He conflates being best, being admired, being chosen, and being loved. His tactical intelligence makes him better at rationalizing emotional avoidance.
3. **Envious of Dante.** The envy is about more than tournament rank: he envies Dante's passionate authenticity and the power Dante has to affect him. He wants to beat Dante and to matter to him. His fixation makes him competitive, possessive, theatrical and sometimes cruel.
4. **Confused about and defensive toward his deeper feelings.** Steffan is attracted to Dante, but suppresses or misnames that attraction as rivalry. He fears the vulnerability involved in admitting desire, needing recognition, or being rejected. Dante's reciprocal attraction is **not** established automatically; it can remain unresolved or be proposed as a mutually chosen alternate route.
5. **Lyra as genuine attraction *and* emotional displacement.** Steffan is attracted to Lyra as an exceptional athlete and glamorous woman. Simultaneously, he pursues her partly as a proxy victory over Dante. Being with her signifies imagined domination of Dante, and he takes pleasure in eliciting Dante's jealousy. She is not merely a sham attraction, and she never stops being a person with her own desires and choices.
6. **Dominating and controlling toward Lyra.** Steffan enjoys directing her, setting the terms of encounters, seeing her respond to his confidence, and sometimes testing how much influence he has. He can cross into possessiveness, manipulating her public image, undermining her independence or turning affection into leverage. These are real harmful traits, not automatically sanitized as flirtation. When they negotiate a consensual private power-play dynamic, that is distinct from attempts to control her life or career.

## Why his domination is dramatically coherent

Steffan's emotional reasoning can run: *Dante has power over my feelings → I cannot bear that → If Lyra chooses me and allows me to lead, I prove I am the one with power → I never have to name what I want from Dante.* This is a proposed narrative psychological model, not a clinical diagnosis; he need not consciously narrate it or understand it correctly.

His confidence may genuinely attract Lyra. The same behavior can make her uncomfortable when he expects her to submit to demands beyond what she has chosen. Different decisions should lead to different scenes: honest but provocative chemistry, controlling manipulation with real consequences, rejection, and a possible late change of behavior. The plot should not imply that sexual assertiveness itself is abusive or that consensual dominance excuses nonconsensual pressure.

## Default canonical storyline

Steffan wins Group C over Lyra and Milano, defeats Dante legitimately in his quarterfinal, and loses his semifinal rematch to Lyra. He begins a genuine romantic affair with Lyra after she leaves Dante, but his initial manipulation and involvement in Varga's engineered encounters come to light; Lyra leaves him on Day 11. Dante has no obligatory romance with Steffan. Steffan is not forgiven by a tournament win or by a public confession.

## Playable alternatives (draft, not ratified pairings)

- He may refrain from pursuing Lyra; they remain rivals, friends or distant colleagues according to other choices.
- He may pursue Lyra honestly, control her, or continue displacing his emotions. Lyra's feelings and ability to say no must be independently authored, not read off a player-selected success score.
- He may recognize, deny or speak aloud his attraction to Dante. Dante may reject him, remain ambivalent or reciprocate after separate voluntary decisions and the resolution of existing commitments. Steffan–Dante is an **optional proposal**, not an assumed canonical couple or a guaranteed “true” ending.
- He may remain manipulative and successful, lose affection without losing athletic ability, gain self-awareness without redemption, or gradually improve through consequences and continued choices.
- Steffan should have nonromantic relationships and a meaningful role in the Nova investigation, using his understanding of broadcast technology and timing.

## Continuity safeguards

- **Not** a story where being attracted to another man caused Steffan's cruelty. His choices around vulnerability, envy and power explain his conduct; orientation does not.
- Lyra can be attracted to confident, dominating behavior and still reject efforts to control her public image, work, friendships and choices. She never becomes the romantic prize attached to a fight outcome.
- Dante is not automatically available or reciprocating, and cannot be replaced with a projection of Steffan's unresolved feelings.
- Varga manipulates opportunities and surveillance; does **not** manufacture love, sexual orientation, consent or match results.
- This character addition does not alter the Day-12 beneficial Nova rescue, Day-13 executive veto and destruction, or Day-30 independent proof of the Kreissack Devroe murder order.
