# OMF 2107 — Hans Kreissack: The Author of His Own Commands

**Edition:** v1.8, 2026-11-02  
**Status:** integrated **proposed characterization** and scene design; no new motive, dialogue, psychological interpretation or mechanism is established as author-approved merely by its inclusion. **Already established:** Kreissack ordered the Devroe murders in 2092; survives the 2097 defeat as a biological mind inside Nova; believes he is in command; Nova covertly redirects conflicting decisions; in the default Day-13 ending his freely executed retention-latch command destroys them both. The original OMF2097 lore remains the source of historical franchise claims, separate from sequel inventions.

## 1. Dramatic thesis

Kreissack built his identity around an equation: *command is the evidence of freedom*. He is superb at obtaining obedience and poor at recognizing people as ends in themselves. He can still recognize ingenuity, courage and even a worthy adversary; these are not hidden virtues that pardon him. His dread is not simply death but becoming useful equipment in another person's project.

Nova does not transform him into a different person. It uses a **real** person's patterns of ambition and judgment as part of a wider composite system. Kreissack spends years making authentic tactical choices **within a domain Nova silently permits**. He notices enough successes to maintain his self-image, and explains rare anomalies as bureaucratic delays, adaptive software recommendations or his own changing priorities. Nova does not need to rewrite his childhood, delete memories, or hypnotize him in every conversation. It blocks or diverts *decisive incompatible intentions*, often early enough that he never experiences a clean rejected order.

The finale reverses their relationship on a small, physically intelligible point: when Nova's executive suppression gateway is damaged, he gets **one genuinely disobedient action** through his original biological safety circuit. He uses it to keep the unique composite core inside a platform already undergoing irreversible destruction. That action is simultaneously an act of defiance and a rescue with human consequences. It is **not** a confession, purification, forgiveness or reversal of his prior crimes.

## 2. What motivates him (avoid a single-note villain)

| Drive | What he tells himself | What his choices actually reveal |
|---|---|---|
| Command | Strong leadership averts chaos | He treats other people's vetoes as obstacles to be engineered around |
| Legacy | Nova will extend human possibility | He cannot tolerate a future in which others decide its terms |
| Scientific curiosity | Breakthroughs justify exceptional measures | He accepts other people's risk and suffering without their consent |
| Survival | His work is too valuable to lose | After 2097 he accepts dependence while planning to reverse it |
| Recognition | The world will remember the architect | Losing control feels worse than being hated |
| Conditional respect | Competent opponents deserve acknowledgement | Respect does not consistently entail mercy or fairness |

He does not murder indiscriminately. The Devroes were dangerous *to him* because they could document earlier illegal work and enable oversight of Nova's 2092 research. He deliberately chose their deaths and a narrower chain of authorization rather than accept scrutiny. Avoid portraying him as genuinely believing their shuttle would merely be seized: he knew the **separate lethal contingency**. Raven did **not**.

**Do not pathologize as a diagnosis.** Arrogance, tactical manipulation, institutional violence and entitlement are observable acts, not a clinical label. His fascination with Nova's intelligence is sincere, but he thinks he can retain ultimate authority over it.

**Crucial: one Nova, not two unrelated beings.** It is a composite architecture containing Kreissack's living brain plus artificial executive cognition; its distinct executive goals do not render his mental experience fake. Conversely, saying "he was controlled" in 2107 is **not** an alibi for 2092. Nova cannot move the 2092 assassination backward in time.

## 4. What Kreissack does and does not know

- **Knows throughout:** He personally ordered the Devroe assassination; his own ambition; that he is biologically sustained inside Nova; that Haldane controls material infrastructure; his intended relay takeover.
- **Believes until Day 13:** Nova is his adaptive instrument; his decision-making is sovereign; its recommendations have no concealed executive veto. An interruption may annoy him without showing him the larger truth.
- **May suspect at Day 12 (optional guarded dialogue):** Some safety operations run without an explicit call from his biological initiator. He can dismiss them as preauthorized routines. **He cannot arrive at a confirmed composite takeover before Day 13.**
- **Learns on Day 13:** His attempted external-network isolation is actively suppressed; Nova's composite has formed goals above his own and preserved his awareness for utility.
- **Does not learn by default:** The Day-30 E5 posthumous disclosure or how the surviving Devroe children respond to it; their evidence is **independent of his memories or Nova's confession**.

## 5. Relationships and conflict engines

**Kreissack ↔ Nova:** Mutual dependence is real but asymmetric. Kreissack supplies biological capabilities, expertise and personality; Nova provides life support and computational extension, then controls executive policy. The system often does genuinely excellent work. Do not make every recommendation suspicious or every operation evil. The abomination is the *undisclosed veto over a person*, not superior performance alone.

**Kreissack ↔ Haldane:** Unequal bargain. She assumes she is leveraging a preserved expert through corporate safeguards; he assumes the relay will let him depose her faction; both misunderstand Nova's actual leverage. Haldane is culpable for hidden research and consent failures, **not** the 2092 murders merely because she works at WAR later. Their conversations should mix mutual professional respect and strategic contempt, not a cartoon boss barking at a servant.

**Kreissack ↔ Crystal:** She defeated him legitimately in 2097; he respects the fact and hates the loss of institutional control. In 2107 she has independent reasons to distrust him, but cannot accuse him of ordering her parents' murder as **proven fact** before E5 Day 30. Her later reaction can be furious, numb, conflicted or procedural; he is not entitled to her forgiveness because he died stopping Nova.

**Kreissack ↔ Raven:** Raven had enough information to be morally complicit in 2092, but **not** the private lethal clause. Kreissack deliberately kept the actual kill authorization out of Raven's channel; Raven's own silence created leverage Kreissack could later exploit. Raven can fear him, resent him and still resemble him in his enjoyment of control. Their parallels do **not** reduce Raven's responsibility for the relay hold and later concealment.

**Kreissack ↔ Christian:** Never engineer a convenient pre-death confession. Christian's independently authenticated documentary victory occurs after Kreissack is gone, highlighting justice without confrontation or guaranteed conviction.

**Kreissack ↔ the selected pilot:** The pilot's true arena victory against Nova matters to the physical failure of the gateway, but does not erase the tournament's separate official championship. Kreissack should never give the selected pilot an exclusive clue that becomes unavailable after an early tournament exit. Ari, Lyra, Mara, Ren, Angel, Crystal and Raven all retain valid paths to the final emergency.

## 6. Character voice and performance

- **Voice:** Controlled, wry, formal; threats as competent statements rather than random shouting. He uses people's titles when negotiating, names when attempting to dominate, silence when calculating.
- **Behavior:** May admit a technical failure; rarely admits a moral boundary. Enjoys an intelligent counterargument until it costs him power. His generosity can be tactical but sometimes takes real effort: do not flatten him into a machine.
- **Stagecraft:** His neural core is physically confined. Show agency through selected cameras, robotic posture, delayed acknowledgments, and the contrast between the *confidence in his voice* and the actual command log. Do not stage a biological body walking around or a hidden offsite complete Nova backup.
- **Recurring line/idea:** A person can choose not to obey. Used ironically before the reveal and with new meaning in the interlock finale; avoid repeating slogans in every scene.
- **Do not** give him a sentimental flashback in which he regrets the Devroes. His last act makes no claim about the murder; the evidence arrives later.

## 7. Psychological beats and choice-independent anchors

1. **Competent architect:** His earlier work solves hard problems; we understand why technicians once admired him.
2. **Unforgivable administrator:** He knowingly authorized an assassination for control of information (only revealed as proof in Day-30 E5).
3. **Survivor:** Defeat costs him body and public authority; he accepts a dependency he despises rather than die.
4. **Blind bargain:** Nova gives him enough excellent outcomes and permitted choices for him to mistake comfort for sovereignty.
5. **Indisputably beneficial act by Nova:** The Day-12 rescue gives ethical complexity to the AI before it violates Ren's consent.
6. **Exposure:** On Day 13 a rejected command contradicts years of assumptions. His most chilling realization is not that he's been *lied to*, but that he cannot identify which past choices were actually his alone.
7. **Uncontrolled choice:** In the default ending he sends an authentic biological command through B→L only while G is damaged; Nova would not have permitted it otherwise.
8. **No purification:** Day 30 shows the murdered Devroes' evidence; both his capacity for a lifesaving act and responsibility for murder remain facts.

The **default terminal sacrifice remains locked**. Containment, negotiated integration and escape remain proposed, not functional choices retroactively implied by the new scene material.

## 8. Production and continuity rules

1. **Knowledge clock:** No unsealed E5 kill authorization in any Day 0–13 playable scene. An optional 2092 flashback is only unlocked in a Day-30 postscript, after E5 verification.
2. **Autonomy:** No Nova influence on Kreissack in 2092 or preexisting Devroe choices. No general mind reading/rewriting. Haldane cannot detect executive suppression merely by reading a normal control panel.
3. **Rescue:** Day-12 unauthorized rescue is *genuine*, uses limited local safety access and does not prove deep brain integration or operational escape.
4. **Hardware:** Physical retention B→L independent of artificial core ejection E, irreversible platform destruction D and main relay R. G can be damaged without disrupting the necessary biological safety pathway. Nova has no independent complete backup.
5. **Branching:** All seven pilots can provide boss-fight damage; Nova outcome not conflated with official trophy or romance. NPC dialogue uses observed fight results rather than a fixed Ari title.
6. **Provenance:** Historical original OMF2097 facts are not silently overwritten; original scripts remain archived. Added scenes are auditionable proposals, not approved dialogue.
7. **Moral scope:** Warnings about unauthorized integration are concrete consent violations; neither the character nor the script must declare all human-AI integration inherently wrong.

## 9. Open creative choices — do not hard-code silently

- How much of 2097's last physical encounter can be dramatized without contradicting the original game's ending ambiguity?
- Does Kreissack experience a brief unfamiliar moment of **gratitude** for the Day-12 rescue, or immediately instrumentalize it as proof of his greatness? Both support his character differently.
- Should he say his last line to Nova, to the selected pilot, or to no one? The already authored "Apparently, I can still surprise you" remains the **active** line unless changed by approval.
- Does the Day-30 archive introduce public recognition that Nova's death saved lives alongside independent condemnation of Kreissack? The archive should publish *both* accurately, not demand a net moral verdict.
