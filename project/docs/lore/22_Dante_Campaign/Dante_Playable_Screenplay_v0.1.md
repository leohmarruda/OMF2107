# ONE MUST FALL 2107 — DANTE VEY: WHAT YOU CANNOT WIN

**Pilot-specific, six-chapter branching screenplay — first draft, Story Pack v2.2.**

**Character direction established by the author:** Dante is more mature and emotionally aware than Steffan, but passionate, proud, angry and capable of deliberate spite after Steffan interferes with his relationship with Lyra. He eventually confronts Steffan and exposes the emotional truth he is denying. Dante recognizes something real; he does not possess omniscient knowledge of Steffan's mind. Steffan's exceptional competence, immature envy, attraction to Dante, genuine attraction to Lyra, and controlling behavior remain established. Dante's own romantic reciprocity toward Steffan is *not* established; it is explored only as an optional branch requiring affirmative choice.

**Play rules:** The established NPC story has Dante initially dating Lyra, losing her to Steffan, beating Jean-Paul and losing to Angel in Group D, and losing to Steffan in the quarterfinal. Actual fights by a selected Dante can alter all sporting outcomes. Every match has exactly one winner. If Dante does not qualify, no knockout is played. A 1–1–1 group *record* can be resolved only by the provisional published seeding, never by a drawn fight. The Nova emergency is separate from the championship. The [CHOICE] markers signal dialogue and relationship state; they do not override combat results, force romantic reciprocity, or rewrite another person's consent.

# CHAPTER 1 — A MAN WHO LOOKS STRAIGHT AT THINGS

*Days 0–1. Theme: perception is not the same as self-control.*

### DV1.1 — The anniversary walkway [MANDATORY]

**INT. WAR ANNIVERSARY COMPLEX — ARRIVAL WALKWAY — NIGHT**

Dante Vey refuses the hover-camera's demand for a second entrance. His hair is untidy, his jacket unfastened, his grin entirely unmarketable. Beside him, Lyra Kisaragi looks spectacular in a silver-backed evening outfit that catches the light when she moves. The flashbulbs don't disturb her; she has conquered larger audiences than this one.

**ALEX:** “The two of you: are you competing together, or competing against each other?”

**LYRA:** “Against. Obviously.”

**DANTE:** “But I'm buying dinner when she beats me.”

**LYRA:** “Expensive dinner.”

Steffan appears, perfectly lit, and begins talking about Pyros' new defensive synchronization while addressing Lyra. His mathematics is excellent. Dante knows it. His timing is calculated to make the camera forget Lyra's own tournament record.

**DANTE:** “She's won three major titles. The machine can wait a second.”

**STEFFAN:** “Protective?”

**LYRA:** “He's correct. That doesn't mean I need protecting.”

**DANTE:** “Fair.”

Steffan's gaze flicks from Lyra to Dante and lingers. Dante notices how quickly Steffan looks away. He smiles, but it is a warning smile.

**CHOICE DV1-C1 — public response:** `playful` (make the trio laugh), `pointed` (return the provocation), or `measured` (put Lyra's achievement back at the center). None dictates Lyra's preferences or tournament outcomes.

### DV1.2 — Private laughter [OPTIONAL ROMANCE / WARM FANFARE]

**INT. PILOT QUARTERS — GARDEN BALCONY — LATE NIGHT**

Lyra kicks off the shoes from the publicity event, revealing tape on a bruised ankle. Dante returns with two cups of water and a packet of ice. She tests his hand against her calf; he offers to stop, and she tells him where it actually hurts.

**LYRA:** “You looked like you wanted to punch him for looking at me.”

**DANTE:** “It wasn't the looking.”

**LYRA:** “He has been looking at you for years.”

Dante laughs despite himself. They are close enough to kiss, but neither needs to use the moment as proof of ownership. Lyra leans in if the active relationship state confirms mutual interest; otherwise she brushes his wrist and talks about tomorrow's training.

**DANTE:** “I know you'll make your own choices.”

**LYRA:** “Knowing that is the easy part.”

**DANTE:** “I didn't promise to be graceful.”

**CHOICE DV1-C2 — Dante's commitment:** `listen` (ask what she wants from the relationship), `deflect` (make a provocative joke), `cling` (ask for an exclusivity pledge she may freely refuse). The scene never locks Lyra into staying. If Dante and Lyra are already separated in an explicitly selected alternative world state, this is a memory, not a present-tense romance scene.

### DV1.3 — The mirror without a reflection [MANDATORY]

**INT. SHREDDER MAINTENANCE BAY — NIGHT**

A junior technician, **MARI SOLIS** (31; proposed new supporting character, independent mechanics contractor, not romantic by default), shows Dante a pre-match actuator diagnostic.

**MARI:** “Your left shoulder's response is half a tick slower. We can compensate. We can't pretend it isn't there.”

**DANTE:** “Tell me that on camera. I'd rather lose honestly than have a machine congratulate me for something it did.”

**MARI:** “That's a nice principle until sponsors call.”

Dante watches Steffan's interview on a side-screen. Steffan is showing off an almost eerily precise clip of Dante's Shredder footwork. For a moment the camera catches Steffan's face before he knows he's on air. It isn't triumph. It looks like yearning. Then his face becomes a smile built for an audience.

**DANTE:** “He never stops watching.”

**MARI:** “Neither do you.”

Dante cannot argue. He turns off the screen.

# CHAPTER 2 — A VICTORY WITH NO ONE TO SHOW

*Days 2–4. Theme: competition deserves its own integrity.*

### DV2.1 — Shredder versus Shadow [MANDATORY MATCH GD3]

**INT. ARENA — QUALIFYING ROUND**

Jean-Paul slips through angles, inviting Dante to overcommit. Dante's first instinct is to charge. Instead he counts the rhythm of Shadow's false openings; the pause before the third is a little too generous.

**DANTE:** “You can lie about where you're going. Not about where you put your weight.”

**PLAY GD3 — DANTE vs JEAN-PAUL.** The default Dante wins decisively. If the player wins, Jean-Paul compliments Dante's patience, irritating him more than an insult. If the player loses, Dante recognizes the trap and congratulates the fighter while furious at himself. No fight changes the fate of Lyra's relationship.

**JEAN-PAUL:** “You've become less predictable.”

**DANTE:** “You mean less angry.”

**JEAN-PAUL:** “I would not go that far.”

### DV2.2 — The people behind the armor [MANDATORY; SECONDARY CHARACTER]

Mari introduces a technician who was reprimanded for reporting inconsistent Shredder sensor exports. WAR is collecting spectator and pilot response markers beyond what the published consent form describes. Mari has only authorized maintenance observations, not the neurobiology records that belong to Angel and Ren's independent investigations.

**MARI:** “If you take the raw log, they'll call it stolen. If you sign my chain-of-custody declaration, they have to explain why ordinary calibration diagnostics are being mirrored into an audience-analysis server.”

**CHOICE DV2-C1 — evidence:** `secure` (help document the anomaly lawfully), `grandstand` (make it an immediate scandal), or `decline` (leave evidence collection to Mari). The anomaly remains available through independent witnesses; Dante's choice changes his credibility and alliances.

### DV2.3 — The audience is not a relationship [OPTIONAL ENSEMBLE / FANFARE]

**INT. PROMOTIONAL ROOFTOP POOL — DAY**

Shirro organizes a publicity event full of athletic demonstrations, swimwear, staged dares and boastful sparring. Lyra dives cleanly from the high platform. Steffan critiques her entry angle, then offers to demonstrate. Dante calls him a coward unless he can do it without a camera.

**SHIRRO:** “I have been told the world's finest athletes are mature adults.”

**MARA:** “You were misinformed.”

Dante chooses whether to join the performance, leave with Mari to inspect a suspicious display cabinet, or step back when he notices Lyra's annoyance. A playful splash or consensual flirt can occur only where relationship state permits it. Mari's respect for him can deepen without becoming a romance; neither she nor Lyra is awarded as a prize for athletic showmanship.

# CHAPTER 3 — HOW TO MAKE SOMEONE LOOK AT YOU

*Days 5–7. Theme: humiliation can be chosen by an enemy, but revenge remains one's own decision.*

### DV3.1 — The fight that wasn't about them [MANDATORY MATCH GD1]

**INT. ARENA — QUALIFYING ROUND**

Angel runs Chronos with alarming precision. Dante's aggression nearly wins him the initiative before Angel changes timing and opens his guard. He recovers; Angel changes again.

**ANGEL:** “Are you fighting me or the person you wish was here?”

**DANTE:** “You want the honest answer?”

**ANGEL:** “The fight is the answer.”

**PLAY GD1 — DANTE vs ANGEL.** Default Dante loses. A selected Dante may legitimately win. Every result is decisive and the actual result appears on the board. In default continuity, Dante wins GD3 and loses GD1, qualifying D2; player alternatives use group rank, including provisional public seeding for circular records.

### DV3.2 — Lyra chooses [MANDATORY; CONDITIONAL]

**INT. HOTEL WALKWAY — EVENING**

Steffan is waiting beside Lyra. The expression on his face is too pleased. Lyra's is complicated and deliberate.

If the Dante–Lyra relationship remains active and Lyra chooses Steffan in the default route, Dante asks once whether she's certain. She is.

**DANTE:** “Did he convince you, or did you decide?”

**LYRA:** “I decided. There are things I want that you can't decide for me.”

Dante swallows. It would be easier to blame Steffan for everything than accept that Lyra is speaking for herself.

**DANTE:** “Then I won't tell people you were stolen.”

Steffan steps forward with a remark designed to make that sound like surrender.

**STEFFAN:** “Very adult of you.”

**DANTE:** “Don't congratulate me for resisting the urge to break your jaw.”

If player-created relationship history instead has Lyra remaining with Dante, leaving both, or never dating Steffan, the same location hosts a confrontation about Steffan's provocations and media exploitation, *not an invented breakup*. Dante and Lyra may also break up for their own reasons, with no Steffan romance. All versions preserve Lyra's freedom to choose.

**CHOICE DV3-C1 — response to loss or pressure:** `accept` (acknowledge her decision), `lash_out` (say something cruel and later owe an apology), `beg` (plead, with no power to reverse her decision). `lash_out` is a real moral failure; choosing it changes whether Lyra will trust him later.

### DV3.3 — A cruel little triumph [MANDATORY; PERSONAL FLAW]

**INT. BACKSTAGE LOADING CORRIDOR — LATE NIGHT**

Dante catches Steffan alone after an edited clip of Lyra leaving him floods the feeds. Steffan is triumphant and strangely unable to hold Dante's gaze.

**DANTE:** “You spent months studying how I move, then you had to find somewhere else to hurt me.”

**STEFFAN:** “You think everything is about you?”

**DANTE:** “No. That is your specialty.”

He sees Steffan's flicker of panic. It makes him angry enough to enjoy having noticed.

**DANTE:** “You kept asking whether she loved you. You never asked whether she was happy.”

**STEFFAN:** “That's an accusation.”

**DANTE:** “It's an observation.”

**CHOICE DV3-C2 — spite:** `needle` (deliberately imply Steffan's real fixation), `withdraw` (walk away before enjoying the harm), `threaten_public_scene` (promise to confront him on camera, damaging trust and later exposing Dante's own vindictiveness). The truth is not an excuse for invasive public humiliation; in the darker branch Dante can still attempt it and face costs.

### DV3.4 — Someone says no [OPTIONAL; SECONDARY-CHARACTER CHEMISTRY]

Mari offers Dante an after-hours workshop tour of Shredder's rebuilt joints. If they are both free adults in this optional future route, she may offer him dinner when the tournament is over. The attraction is quiet: grease on her wrist, a dry joke about his inability to follow instructions, a moment where he deliberately doesn't perform for an audience.

**MARI:** “I like you when you're not trying to win an argument that ended yesterday.”

**DANTE:** “That sounds like once a year.”

**MARI:** “You could try twice.”

This is a **proposed** adult romance, never a default commitment and never conditioned on Mari handing over evidence. A nonromantic professional partnership is fully playable and equally valuable.

# CHAPTER 4 — THE MATCH THEY BOTH WANTED

*Days 8–10. Theme: one can beat an opponent and still fail to settle the grievance.*

### DV4.1 — Official standings [MANDATORY]

Dante checks the board. Two qualifying wins place him D1 in Q4 against C2. One win normally places him D2 in Q3 against C1; in a three-way 1–1–1 situation the published provisional order is Angel, Dante, Jean-Paul. Zero wins eliminates him at the group stage, with no invented quarterfinal. The *player's* outcomes determine the real bracket and winner.

### DV4.2 — Quarterfinal or honest elimination [CONDITIONAL]

**IF QUALIFIED:** The official board announces the actual Q3 or Q4 opponent. In the default timeline it is Steffan at Q3. They enter Shredder and Pyros under a roar so loud the arena flooring vibrates.

**STEFFAN:** “How long have you rehearsed this?”

**DANTE:** “Long enough to know it won't make either of us happy.”

**STEFFAN:** “Then why come?”

**DANTE:** “Because you're good enough that beating you means something. The rest isn't your trophy.”

**PLAY Q3/Q4 IF QUALIFIED.** Default Steffan wins, decisively. If Dante wins, it is a genuine victory and he advances; Steffan cannot compel Lyra to return or erase Dante's anger. If Dante loses, he is eliminated from the official tournament but remains central to the investigation. If the actual opponent is not Steffan, use fresh match-specific dialogue rather than the grudge dialogue.

**IF ELIMINATED:** Dante joins Mari and Mara to challenge a dangerous crowd-control test that ignores worker safety. He does not receive a fake quarterfinal, and the story does not imply his investigation was purchased by a victory.

### DV4.3 — A different kind of evidence [MANDATORY]

An audience-control technician tells Mari that an old emergency relay continues to accept priority instructions using a retired device certificate. Dante can help secure a physical calibration record with witnessed signatures. Angel and Ren, independently, verify **that Kreissack is biologically alive** through properly obtained evidence. Dante does not impersonate a medical professional or read private neural data.

**DANTE:** “He told us he was a ghost.”

**ANGEL:** “He was always a person. That makes the experiments worse, not better.”

**CHOICE DV4-C1:** `protect_technician` / `leak_too_soon` / `avoid_risk`. Disclosure strategy affects trust and witness safety; independent proof of Kreissack's survival persists.

### DV4.4 — The worst part of winning [OPTIONAL; DEPENDS ON BRACKET]

If Dante actually beats Steffan in the quarterfinal, Steffan approaches him in the corridor afterward. Dante could make a grand declaration. Instead he recognizes the disappointment on Steffan's face. He doesn't look like a man who has lost a woman. He looks like a man who has lost a chance to be seen by one specific person.

**DANTE:** “That fight mattered. What you did to Lyra matters more.”

If Steffan beats Dante, Dante watches the replay once; he can smash the screen, call Mari, or go running until he can speak without hurting someone else. Every branch preserves real match outcomes and allows his anger to be ugly.

# CHAPTER 5 — SAY IT TO MY FACE

*Days 11–12. Theme: recognizing another person's secret is not a license to use it however one wishes.*

### DV5.1 — A document, not a conspiracy theory [MANDATORY DAY 11]

Jean-Paul and Leena arrange independent authentication of Varga's unauthorized emotional study: opportunistic scheduling, selective media edits, and intrusive observation. They deliver protected findings to Lyra whether or not Dante cooperated. The study did **not** induce anyone's affection and did **not** rig the tournament.

If Lyra dated Steffan, she learns he initially pursued her partly to overpower Dante. She confronts him and ends their relationship in the default timeline. If they never dated, she confronts the manipulation without a fictitious breakup.

Dante sees Lyra on the practice deck as the news becomes public. He does not ask to be taken back.

**DANTE:** “I want to say I told you so. I'm trying not to.”

**LYRA:** “You can want to say something and still decide not to.”

**DANTE:** “I'm learning from an expert.”

If he previously lashed out, he can apologize without requesting forgiveness. If Lyra remained with him in a player-specific branch, they may discuss what trust looks like after the scandal; that is not a guaranteed romance ending.

### DV5.2 — The truth he wanted to hurt Steffan with [MANDATORY CONFRONTATION DAY 11]

**INT. EMPTY TRAINING GALLERY — NIGHT**

The gallery is lined with discarded publicity sets. The walls display frozen images from Pyros and Shredder: two fighters squared against each other at different ages, their names in huge type, almost a shared brand. Steffan has come to erase his own promotional marks. Dante is waiting.

**STEFFAN:** “Come to celebrate the collapse of my private life?”

**DANTE:** “Not yet. I thought I'd give you a chance to lie to me first.”

**STEFFAN:** “Lyra and I cared for each other.”

**DANTE:** “I believe that. It's the one thing you keep saying that I don't think is a lie.”

Steffan stops stripping the sponsor tape from a wall.

**DANTE:** “You didn't chase her because you couldn't feel anything. You chased her because you felt too much, about too many things, and she was the only part of it you thought you could control.”

**STEFFAN:** “You think you can diagnose me after losing a match?”

**DANTE:** “I noticed before the match. You study every move I make, you watch who I love, and when you get what you said you wanted, you look to see whether *I* saw it.”

A tremor of anger crosses Steffan's face.

**DANTE:** “You envy me. You want to beat me. Some days you want to be me. And there's something else you've been too frightened to name.”

**STEFFAN:** “Don't finish that sentence.”

The silence finishes it for him.

**DANTE:** “You wanted me to look at you. So you made her into the message.”

**STEFFAN:** “You know nothing about what I—”

**DANTE:** “I know enough to see the difference between wanting someone and needing to own their choice.”

Steffan approaches until he is inches away. Dante does not step back. For one dangerous second, both are less concerned with the audience than at any point in the tournament.

**STEFFAN:** “And what is it you want from me, Dante?”

Dante's expression changes. This is no longer a rhetorical question.

**CHOICE DV5-C1 — confrontation tone:**

- `merciless`: **DANTE:** “I wanted you to feel as small as you made her feel.” He smiles when Steffan flinches. **STEFFAN:** “You finally found a way to beat me outside the arena.” **DANTE:** “Yes. And I hate how good it feels.” Dante has exposed a truth *to Steffan*, but he has also made a weapon of it; trust suffers.
- `clear`: **DANTE:** “I wanted you to stop hurting her, and stop lying to yourself. I don't know what you think I should do with the rest.” Steffan's reply is a quieter denial: “There is no rest.” Dante answers, “Then why can't you look at me?”
- `vulnerable`: **DANTE:** “I was furious when she left. I was more furious that you used her to reach me. Some part of me still wants your respect, and that makes me angry at myself.” **STEFFAN:** “You think I respect you?” **DANTE:** “I think you've never known what to do with how much you do.”

All variants retain **Dante's insight and the confrontation**. In `merciless`, he chooses spite and has to live with it. In `clear`, he states facts without resolving their relationship. In `vulnerable`, he takes a personal risk but does not establish romantic reciprocation unless the player separately chooses that route later. Steffan may deny, admit confusion, or ask for time; his inner attraction is canon, but his exact admission is reactive prose, not a forced love confession. He has no claim on Dante or Lyra.

**OPTIONAL PUBLIC FALLOUT DV5.2b:** If Dante previously threatened a public scene, leaked imagery can circulate and complicate both careers. Dante may acknowledge he vindictively exposed intimate implications or refuse to acknowledge it. He is not rewarded with popularity, romance, or an automatic win for humiliating a rival. The default scene stays private; its emotional disclosure is not equivalent to publicly labeling Steffan's orientation.

### DV5.3 — The rescue nobody ordered [MANDATORY DAY 12, BEFORE SEMIFINAL]

A safety emergency traps crew members near the Nova wing. An automated intervention saves them with no contemporaneous human command. Dante helps carry a technician clear of a pressure door; Mari and Angel preserve contemporaneous engineering records. Ren confirms that the life-saving act was real, not staged. Kreissack later takes interest in the recording, but his command did not initiate the rescue.

**DANTE:** “A machine can save someone. That isn't a license to make every decision for them.”

### DV5.4 — A semifinal, if earned [CONDITIONAL DAY 12]

If Dante actually won his quarterfinal, `S_CD` is playable against the other C/D quarterfinal winner. In the default route he is already eliminated and watches the actual Lyra–Steffan semifinal, which Lyra wins. If his bracket changes, the actual contestants and winner change; there is no fictional rematch to pay off the rivalry.

**LYRA (if Steffan is her actual opponent):** “Watch the fight. Not what you think it means.”

**DANTE:** “I'm trying.”

Dante can cheer for Lyra's skill, decline to watch, or see Steffan's loss with petty pleasure. None restores Lyra to him. If Dante is himself a semifinalist, his performance stands on its own rather than being framed as a romantic contest.

# CHAPTER 6 — THE PERSON AFTER THE FIGHT

*Day 13 and aftermath. Theme: anger may be justified; how one uses it determines who one becomes.*

### DV6.1 — The real championship [MANDATORY; CONDITIONAL MATCH]

The arena announces the genuine `S_AB` and `S_CD` winners. Dante plays the official final **only if** he won his qualifying position, quarterfinal and semifinal. If he loses at any stage, he witnesses the finalists who actually advanced. Default final: Ari defeats Lyra. Dante cannot receive a trophy because the script wants emotional closure.

If Dante wins the official title, he looks for Steffan in the crowd, then deliberately raises his arm toward the technicians who kept Shredder safe. If he loses, he may congratulate the victor or retreat bitterly before returning to help with the emergency. A romance choice cannot change the scoreboard.

### DV6.2 — Nova's veto [MANDATORY, AFTER OFFICIAL FINAL]

The complex enters emergency lockdown. Ren and Angel bring their neural and archival evidence; Mara and Mari coordinate workers; Dante uses Shredder's real movement diagnostics to help isolate a relay actuator. Kreissack appears alive. He attempts to issue an instruction to Nova, but the command has no effect. Nova's conditional override of his decisions becomes undeniable.

**DANTE:** “You were watching the one man who believed everybody else belonged to him.”

**ANGEL:** “Don't make the metaphor do the engineering. Help me close the gateway.”

Dante does. The gateway is damaged, the relay isolated. Kreissack's control partly returns; he chooses the physical retention latch. The single composite core is destroyed during the escape attempt. Kreissack's final line is unchanged:

**KREISSACK:** “Apparently, I can still surprise you.”

This is the same physical event seen in the other nine campaigns. The Nova encounter is an emergency confrontation, never an extra official tournament round. Dante knows neither the authenticated Devroe order nor its Day-30 source yet.

### DV6.3 — A relationship is not a trophy [MANDATORY AFTERMATH]

**INT. QUIET TRAINING DECK — AFTER THE LOCKDOWN**

Lyra sits alone, reviewing her semifinal footage without sound. Her analysis is all footwork, timing and discipline. Dante brings her water and places it within reach rather than sitting uninvited.

**DANTE:** “I used to think I knew what would make you happy.”

**LYRA:** “You knew a few things.”

**DANTE:** “Enough to make a nuisance of myself.”

If Dante accepted her choice and has not attacked her publicly, the conversation is warm. If he lashed out, it is harder, and she may decline to speak to him. If they remained together on an alternate path, they speak as partners who have survived a difficult week, not as if their commitment had been freshly earned by a tournament win.

### DV6.4 — Optional Steffan reckoning [AFTER FINALE; ADULTS; ROMANCE NOT DEFAULT]

**INT. EMPTY SPARRING ROOM — NIGHT**

Steffan waits without cameras or uniforms. The confrontation has changed what can be denied, not what can be forgiven.

**STEFFAN:** “When you said those things, you wanted to hurt me.”

**DANTE:** “Sometimes. Yes.”

**STEFFAN:** “You were right about more than I wanted you to be.”

Dante recognizes the precision of that answer. It is the first time Steffan has refused to hide behind virtuosity.

On the **professional-truce branch**, they agree to compete without dragging others into their contest. On the **estrangement branch**, Dante says he wants no further private relationship. On an **optional future reciprocal attraction branch**, Dante may admit that their rivalry has always contained a personal charge for him too, but this is only a *possible* choice, never an established fact. No kiss or relationship is granted immediately; any future romance requires time, both being available, acknowledgement of Lyra's treatment, no coercive-control record without substantive repair, Dante's informed active desire, and Steffan's freely expressed desire.

**DANTE:** “I won't spend my life being your mirror.”

**STEFFAN:** “Then what would you be?”

**DANTE:** “Ask me when you've learned to look at another person.”

### DV6.5 — Alternate personal endings [AFTER FINALE]

- **`solo_rebuild` (default):** Dante returns to Shredder training, competing to be excellent rather than to make someone regret leaving. He remains passionate, capable of anger, and finally a little less governed by it.
- **`lyra_future_proposal`:** Following time, independent mutual decisions and a repaired relationship, Dante and Lyra can consider reconnecting. Her return is never his reward for unmasking Steffan; if she declines, he respects it.
- **`steffan_future_proposal`:** After a substantial interval and substantive accountability, both men may voluntarily explore attraction. Dante is not Steffan's prize, therapist, or proof of redemption. Lyra's previous relationship is resolved first.
- **`mari_future_proposal`:** A modest adult romance with Mari is possible only if she is free, chooses it herself, and Dante's Shredder contracts do not give either party professional leverage over the other. Otherwise they remain colleagues and friends.
- **`vindictive_isolation`:** If he prefers humiliating Steffan and refuses repair, Dante may win matches and lose trust. He is not made a villain or secretly rewarded with another person's affection.
- **`professional_truce`:** Dante and Steffan keep the rivalry alive as athletes, stripped of the claim that one can possess the other's private world.

All paths leave Lyra's independent career and sporting achievements intact.

### DV6.6 — The evidence that arrives late [DAY 30; OPTIONAL EPILOGUE]

Christian and Crystal receive independently authenticated evidence that Kreissack ordered the 2092 Devroe murders. Dante hears a public account only after that authentication, not in the Day-13 finale. He offers to stand as a witness for the independent technician he helped protect, without pretending his own rivalry was the center of the conspiracy.

**DANTE:** “The real world doesn't stop because we finish a fight.”

## Editorial status and production notes

**Established by author:** Dante is comparatively mature and perceptive, but passionate, easily angered and capable of spite, especially toward Steffan for using Lyra. His eventual confrontation exposes Steffan's true fixation and denied attraction. Steffan is exceptionally competent and genuinely attracted to Lyra, yet exploits the relationship to imagine power over Dante.

**Proposed, not fixed:** Dialogue, Dante's precise reaction to the confrontation, any reciprocal attraction to Steffan, a later Lyra reconciliation, new adult technician Mari Solis and any romance with her, and all epilogue details. Independent friendship and solo routes remain meaningful.

**Continuity locks:** No drawn fights; group standings resolution provisional. No romantic flags influence fights, rescue, or the final. Lyra is never controlled by Steffan or Dante. Varga's wrongdoing changes encounters and media but not anyone's genuine attraction or fights. Day-11 misconduct revelation precedes Day-12 independent rescue, which precedes any semifinal. The actual final precedes the Day-13 Nova confrontation. Kreissack's signed murder order is authenticated Day 30. The same unique Nova/Kreissack core is destroyed in each default campaign.
