# ONE MUST FALL 2107 — STEFFAN THOMAS: THE PRICE OF APPLAUSE

**Six-chapter pilot-specific cinematic screenplay — first draft v0.1 / Story Pack v2.1**  
**Editorial status:** Steffan's intelligence, competence, envy of Dante, emotional immaturity, denied attraction, displacement onto Lyra and possessive/dominating impulses are established character direction. Specific scenes, psychological interpretation and optional Steffan–Dante romance remain proposed story writing. All three adults have agency. The default competition and Lyra's default breakup are preserved; player branches never rig matches.

**Dramatic premise.** He can infer an opponent's strategy from a fractional delay in the ankle actuator. He cannot admit why Dante laughing with someone else makes his stomach tighten. He tells himself he wants Lyra because she is magnificent; that's true, but also incomplete. She is the person Dante loves, and possessing her would prove, in Steffan's mind, that even Dante's most private world can be conquered. Steffan prefers the clean language of domination because it spares him the frightening possibility that what he actually wants from Dante is recognition—and perhaps love.

**Branch shorthand:** `[MANDATORY]` in every playthrough; `[OPTIONAL]` requires choices or mutual interest; `[IF ...]` is a gate evaluated from the route dispatcher. `GC1` Steffan–Lyra, `GC2` Steffan–Milano, `Q3/Q4` official quarterfinal as ranked, `S_CD` semifinal, `FINAL` official championship. Fight winners always come from actual player inputs, not romance flags. Each qualifying, knockout and championship match has a decisive winner; a circular 1–1–1 *standings* group is not a drawn fight. The Nova boss is not an official tournament fight.

## Writer's constraints and unresolved creative approvals

1. Steffan is a **skilled, intelligent, competent antagonist in personal relationships**, not an incompetent clown. His tactical predictions are usually insightful; player defeats are earned.
2. His envy and concealed attraction to Dante are not reducible to “he's secretly gay, therefore cruel.” Emotional immaturity, grandiosity and the use of domination to avoid vulnerability produce the cruelty; same-sex attraction does not.
3. Lyra's attraction to his confidence and optional consensual taking-the-lead dynamic **never grants him control over her body, image, career, relationships, or tournament results**. When he oversteps, he is doing harm, whether or not he thinks it feels romantic.
4. **Default:** romantic scandal → Lyra breaks up on Day 11 → Lyra legitimately beats Steffan in the Day-12 semifinal. Steffan and Dante do not automatically become lovers.
5. Dante's default attraction to Steffan is **nonromantic**; a romance is an **author-approved optional direction that the player can develop over time**, not a built-in secret mutual crush or automatic reward. Its precise dialogue and ending remain editorial proposals.
6. Varga interfered with social circumstances and consent, not the authenticity of attraction or competition. Nova's independent rescue is Day 12; executive override fully revealed Day 13; Kreissack's 2092 order is authenticated Day 30 only.
7. The official championship and Nova emergency confrontation are entirely separate. Off-bracket NPC winners and circular group rankings remain provisional as in v2.0.
8. The chapter outlines above are cinematic writing, **not proof that all 32 tournament input patterns have been manually playtested**. The companion validator tests structural dispatch and constraints only.
