# Devroe case — screenplay integration packet v1.5

**Purpose:** full alternative scene dialogue and knowledge-limited dispatch for active Raven v0.2 and Crystal v0.3. This is an authored scene packet, not exposition that characters magically know in Chapter 1. Timing follows the shared event ledger.

## D1 — A locked record is not an alibi (Raven R3.1 / Crystal C3.4; Days 6–8)

**INT. NEUTRAL FORENSICS OFFICE — DAY**

A technician sets two independent recordings on the table. The first is a civilian station log: a fourteen-minute *protected transfer* request. The second is an insurer's spool of the same relay suppression. Both bear Raven's authorization credential. One of the windows is narrower than the other; the traces overlap where the shuttle tried to call for help.

**CHRISTIAN:** You did not just hide a document after the accident. You cut off a way to ask for help before it.

**RAVEN:** I authorized a security hold. The order said an interception, not an execution.

**CRYSTAL:** You knew who was on the shuttle?

**RAVEN:** Yes.

Raven hates that she does not look away. He had imagined ten years of confession and somehow expected to control the room when it happened.

**CRYSTAL:** That means you knew you could be putting them in danger.

**RAVEN:** I knew it was a risk.

**CHRISTIAN:** And you let us spend fifteen years calling it an accident.

Raven's choice to confess, minimize, or deny changes his statements. **The two archives are authenticated regardless**. Crystal and Christian are allowed to stop the meeting. Neither must give him a private audience, and a romantic scene cannot substitute for testimony.

## D2 — The flight that was made to fail (Crystal C4.3 / Raven R4.3; Days 8–10)

**INT. PUBLIC TECHNICAL HEARING — NIGHT**

Three projections show the same approach trajectory, reconstructed independently. The normal course is a slender line turning toward Earth. The actual command veers away into an unsustainable attitude change. It was not a malfunction caused by random cosmic debris. A WAR-contracted maintenance credential issued through an Iron Fist-connected intermediary had inserted the command before departure.

**FORENSIC ENGINEER:** This does not identify the person who typed the lethal instruction. It identifies a planned unauthorized intervention, and the human chain that let it reach the shuttle.

**CHRISTIAN:** Was the relay suppression what killed them?

**ENGINEER:** The flight-control attack caused the fatal deviation. We can show the distress channel was blocked and there was an opportunity to reverse that block. We cannot prove that a restored signal would have saved the ship.

A younger Christian might have shouted. Now he watches the trace twice, until he understands it.

**CRYSTAL:** I want the public record to keep those conclusions separate. My parents were killed. I refuse to accuse anyone of a different act just because the truth is awful enough.

Raven's counsel tries to call this exculpatory. Crystal turns to him.

**CRYSTAL:** He knew I had questions. He knew my brother did. He made us pay for his silence.

The full emergency-message receipt is not introduced until the next independently authenticated scene. No one attributes the sabotage to Nova's autonomous 2107 behavior.

## D3 — The minute he chose (Raven R5.1 fallback or new R5.1B / Crystal C5.2B; Days 9–11, after the independent receipt is authenticated)

**INT. RECORDED DEPOSITION ROOM — AFTERNOON**

The dual-custody receipt records that the crew requested restoration, that Raven's account received the alert, and that the authorization to remove suppression remained unused. The precise rescue probability cannot be derived from it.

**INVESTIGATOR:** When the request arrived, what did you think would happen?

**RAVEN:** If I lifted the hold, command would know the operation was illegal. I thought the ship was still under control.

**INVESTIGATOR:** Did you know what might happen if you kept it?

**RAVEN:** Yes.

For once he does not enjoy having the last word. He could still deny seeing the alert, but the acknowledgement mark is his. In a refusal branch, independent experts reconstruct the same facts without his admission; his lawyer and Crystal still see the record.

**CHRISTIAN:** You were not the person who changed the flight path. You were the person who heard them asking for help and chose the people giving you orders.

**RAVEN:** I chose myself.

This line is his best honest answer. It is not an absolution.

## D4 — The desire that survives judgment (Crystal C5.3, R5.3; optional, Day 12)

**INT. QUIET OBSERVATION CORRIDOR — NIGHT**

Crystal has read the evidence. Raven leaves the path to the exit open. She feels the familiar pull to stop when he says her name. She stops because she decides to, then resents that the distinction is hard to feel.

**CRYSTAL:** If you wanted me to, I know you could make this harder. You like that you could.

**RAVEN:** I do.

**CRYSTAL:** My parents asked for help. You let the request sit there. Do not tell me how much you want me until you understand why I don't trust you.

**RAVEN:** I don't expect you to forgive me.

**CRYSTAL:** I don't know if I'll ever want to. That's not the same as what I want when you get close.

This scene **does not unlock a kiss, a private command sequence or an intimate encounter on Day 12**. Both walk away without a romantic promise. Any romantic D/s ending happens *weeks later*, after case evidence is delivered independently and Crystal freely chooses an informed possibility. On coercive Raven routes, Crystal does not attend: she sends a no-contact notice through counsel.

## D5 — Two accounts, one irreversible act (Crystal C6.4 / Raven R6.4; weeks later)

**INT. INDEPENDENT OVERSIGHT HEARING — DAY**

The hearing publishes separate findings: the deliberate flight-control attack; Raven's security hold before launch; his refusal to restore it after the distress request; and the later falsification. The attribution of the lethal command to a specific person and the final criminal dispositions remain contested. The relatives' right to obtain the record is not conditioned on accepting any settlement.

**CRYSTAL:** I can wish he had been a different man. I can't change what he chose.

**RAVEN (if fully testifying):** I didn't give the command that killed them. I authorized the blackout. I knew they were aboard. I saw the request. I left it in place. And I buried the proof.

**CHRISTIAN:** That's the first time you've said every part aloud.

**RAVEN:** It isn't enough.

**CHRISTIAN:** No.

In denial or obstruction routes, Raven is not allowed a false confession line; independent documents establish the historical findings. A verdict is not automatically rendered.

## D6 — The ending cannot be purchased (optional epilogues)

**Estrangement:** Crystal leaves the hearing even though Raven's voice still affects her. Christian does not congratulate her for 'choosing strength'; he simply asks what she wants for dinner. Raven can still be attracted, bitter and unrepentant.

**Professional distance:** Raven gives useful Nova testimony under counsel and receives neither forgiveness nor romance. Crystal can work beside him on a narrowly defined public-interest task with neutral oversight; he does not have her address or private schedule.

**Conditional post-hearing D/s:** Crystal, fully briefed and already represented by separate counsel, requests a meeting on neutral ground. Raven has testified, has not exploited her, and accepts that no intimacy changes the investigation or its consequences. She explicitly says she will continue to testify. The chemistry is unmistakable, but the private dynamic is an **independently chosen boundary-defined relationship**, never recompense for testimony. If she declines, there is no second attempt. An on-screen embrace or kiss is possible after mutual consent; privacy and more intense intimacy remain offscreen. A guilty finding in later proceedings would be handled as a separate material development, never magically erased.
