# MECHA LINK 2100
## Consolidated Mecha Design Guide
**Version 1.5 — November 17, 2026**  
**Status:** Working design reference  
**Scope:** 17 candidate mechas, visual direction, mechanical anatomy, combat identity, and tournament compatibility.

---

# 1. Project Overview

*Mecha Link 2100* is a futuristic one-on-one fighting game featuring advanced combat robots operated remotely by human pilots.

The tournament showcases technologies relevant to military, industrial, and extraterrestrial applications. Mechas may use humanoid, animal-inspired, or entirely original mechanical anatomies.

The game combines the visual spectacle of futuristic robotic combat with an emphasis on coherent mechanical functionality.

### Core Principles

1. **Distinctive silhouettes:** Every mecha should be recognizable from its outline alone.
2. **Functional anatomy:** Structures should have understandable mechanical purposes.
3. **Stylistic exaggeration:** Proportions may be dramatic without abandoning physical plausibility.
4. **Diverse architectures:** Humanoid designs coexist with quadrupeds, arthropods, flying machines, serpentine robots, and unconventional locomotion systems.
5. **Unified combat controls:** All mechas share the same inputs, regardless of anatomy.
6. **Human tactical control:** Pilots make combat decisions; automation manages motor coordination and stabilization.
7. **Competitive counterplay:** Every major strength must have meaningful limitations.
8. **Technological plausibility:** Advanced materials, propulsion, and controls are permitted, but capabilities must be internally consistent.
9. **Originality:** All final designs, names, animations, and assets must be independently developed.

### Roster Scope

There are currently **17 candidate mechas**.

The intended initial release remains approximately **10 playable mechas plus one story boss**, subject to revision. The boss has not been selected or designed.

No current candidate is guaranteed inclusion in the release roster.

# 2. Visual Art Direction

## 2.1 Overall Style

**Stylized futuristic science fiction with grounded mechanical realism.**

The preferred visual quality is comparable to premium, semi-realistic science-fiction game concept art.

The design should emphasize:

- Clean, readable overall silhouettes.
- Bold yet purposeful mechanical proportions.
- Sophisticated hard-surface construction.
- Recognizable joints, actuators, structural connections, and protective systems.
- Advanced industrial materials.
- Clear focal areas such as sensor clusters, weapons, and primary manipulators.
- Strong differences in size, posture, and movement.
- Controlled use of illumination and energy effects.

The goal is not documentary engineering realism. Rather, designs should make physical and technological sense within a coherent advanced-technology setting.

## 2.2 Shape Language

Each mecha should have one dominant geometric identity.

| Geometry | Typical association |
|---|---|
| Broad wedges | Charging, stability, frontal impact |
| Tall vertical masses | Monumentality, reach, intimidation |
| Sharp diagonals | Speed, aggression, precision |
| Circular forms | Rotation, mobility, unconventional engineering |
| Flat horizontal profiles | Gliding, hovering, low-profile movement |
| Segmented curves | Flexibility, serpentine locomotion |
| Radial structures | Multidirectional stability and mobility |
| Large asymmetry | Specialized attack and defense |

These associations are artistic guidelines, not mandatory rules.

## 2.3 Materials

Suggested material families include:

- Advanced metallic alloys.
- High-strength ceramic and composite armor.
- Flexible reinforced coverings around joints.
- Hardened transparent sensor housings.
- Replaceable protective panels.
- High-temperature coatings.
- Visible bearing assemblies and articulated load-bearing structures.

Surface detailing should reinforce physical function rather than add unnecessary complexity.

## 2.4 Color and Lighting

Each mecha should receive a distinct palette, generally containing:

- One dominant structural color.
- One secondary armor or machinery color.
- One restricted accent color.
- A small amount of functional illumination.

Large forms must remain readable without color.

Lighting and effects must not obscure important movement cues or vulnerable components.

## 2.5 Mechanical Credibility

Every design should answer:

- Where are its principal loads transmitted?
- How are its joints actuated?
- How does it accelerate, turn, stop, and recover from impacts?
- How does it maintain stability across different gravity environments?
- Where are its vulnerable components?
- How can damaged parts remain operational?
- How do its weapons physically perform their attacks?

Exact engineering calculations are not required at the concept stage, but obvious contradictions should be avoided.

# 3. Shared Combat System

## 3.1 Basic Format

Combat occurs on a two-dimensional fighting plane with three-dimensional rendering.

Matches feature rounds, health, stun, knockdowns, aerial attacks, and short combos.

Two principal modes use the same moves:

- **Classic:** Story and Arcade fighting rules.
- **Career:** Tournament rules with persistent component damage until repair.

No super, heat, rage, or special-energy meter is planned.

The game's existing health and stun systems remain in force.

## 3.2 Attributes

Each mecha has three attributes ranging from 1 to 10, totaling **18 points**:

| Attribute | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Power (P) | Attack damage and impact |
| Agility (A) | Speed, mobility, repositioning |
| Resistance (R) | Durability and stability under attack |

The attribute scale is established, but individual roster values remain provisional.

Move-specific parameters ultimately determine actual combat behavior.

## 3.3 Universal Controls

| Input | Function |
|---|---|
| Forward / Back | Advance / retreat; backward direction guards |
| Up / Down | Jump or rise / crouch |
| Down + Back | Low guard |
| S1 | Light punch-function attack |
| S2 | Heavy punch-function attack |
| C1 | Light kick-function attack |
| C2 | Heavy kick-function attack |
| S1 + C1 | Universal grab |
| Down + S1 + S2 | Signature ability |
| Quarter-circle forward + S1/S2 | Offensive special family |
| Quarter-circle backward + C1/C2 | Mobility/control special family |

Every mecha receives standing, crouching, and airborne versions of S1, S2, C1, and C2.

The four-button system describes **combat functions, not literal human limbs**.

For example, a serpentine robot may execute a kick-function attack using its tail, while a wheel-based robot may attack using its rotating rim.

The intended base repertoire contains 12 normal attacks, one universal grab, and three special-move families, although the exact number of distinct special variants remains a design detail.

## 3.4 Component Damage

All mechas use four functional damage slots.

| Slot | Effect of damage |
|---|---|
| Arms | Punch-function attacks become weaker |
| Legs | Walking speed decreases; jumping remains available |
| Torso | Incoming damage increases |
| Head | Stun recovery becomes longer |

Each component has three states:

**Operational → Damaged → Critical**

A Critical component never removes a move or disables its associated input.

The physical implementation of these slots depends on the mecha's anatomy. A wheel, tail, wing, or thruster assembly can represent the locomotion slot.

## 3.5 Special Traits

Five established trait categories exist:

- **Camouflage:** reduces visual predictability without eliminating reasonable reaction opportunities.
- **Counter:** rewards correctly timed defensive actions.
- **Flight:** offers limited powered aerial mobility.
- **Modular Weapons:** allows a fixed repertoire of different integrated weapons; no live weapon swapping.
- **Energy Shield:** provides active protection that must have meaningful counterplay.

Traits do not require additional buttons or resource meters.

## 3.6 Power Subtypes

The established twelve power subtypes are:

**Impact, Penetration, Cutting, Explosive, Thermal, Electric, Electromagnetic, Energy, Corrosive, Kinetic, Concussive, Structural.**

These primarily influence existing damage, stun, guard pressure, and component-damage systems.

They do not automatically authorize persistent status effects, new combat resources, or bypasses of armor and defense.

# 4. Tournament and Physical Constraints

The following tournament decisions apply to the entire candidate roster.

## 4.1 Human Piloting

Combat is remotely piloted by humans.

Automatic systems may coordinate multiple limbs, stabilize the machine, and execute predefined movements.

They may not independently choose targets, select tactical actions, aim intelligently, or execute autonomous evasive strategies.

## 4.2 Arenas and Gravity

Mechas must be operable in standardized competition arenas on:

| Location | Approximate natural gravity |
|---|---:|
| Earth | 1.000 g |
| Mars | 0.378 g |
| Moon | 0.165 g |

The controlled atmosphere is standardized to approximately 1 atm. Detailed composition and density remain unspecified.

Propulsion systems must not depend exclusively on atmospheric oxygen or aerodynamic lift.

## 4.3 Universal Descent Module

All competing mechas use a standardized Universal Descent Module (MUD).

During downward vertical motion, it supplies supplemental downward acceleration in reduced-gravity environments, producing approximately **0.7 g effective descent acceleration** where natural gravity falls below that level.

It does not provide tactical steering, horizontal attacks, or additional targeting functions.

The existing proposal applies this assistance to all qualifying descents, including being launched into the air by an opponent.

## 4.4 Levitation and Flight

Standard low-level levitation is permitted up to **20 cm** above the ground.

Limited powered flight is allowed under universal constraints:

- Maximum vertical displacement is tied to the standardized jump-height reference, \(H_J\).
- Active vertical propulsion outside the low-levitation zone is limited to two accumulated seconds.
- The allowance resets after one continuous second within the low-levitation zone.
- Passive ballistic motion does not consume powered-flight time.

The numerical value of \(H_J\) is not finalized. A 2 m value has been used experimentally.

## 4.5 Ranged Weapons

Ranged projectiles are permitted, including fixed integrated guns and discrete energy shots.

However:

- Continuous energy beams are prohibited.
- Explosive projectiles are prohibited.
- Detached auxiliary combat drones are prohibited.
- Autonomous targeting and target-seeking intelligence are prohibited.

The Explosive power subtype remains usable through other legal attack mechanisms.

## 4.6 Projectile Activation Limits

The current rule provides a shared pool of six projectile activations across a mecha's weapons.

Each activation uses one charge, which individually becomes available again one second later. Recharge timers operate in parallel.

The purpose is to restrict repeated projectile activation without introducing a separately managed tactical resource for the player.

The engine must clearly distinguish one homologated attack activation from a sequence of separate shots.

# 6. Individual Mecha Design Profiles

## 01 — Rhino Bastion

**Original archetype:** Heavy Tank  
**Role:** Armored frontal assault  
**Attributes:** 7 Power / 2 Agility / 9 Resistance  
**Trait:** Energy Shield  
**Power subtypes:** Impact, Concussive  
**Concept status:** Retained

### Anatomy and Appearance

A low, massive quadruped combining rhinoceros-inspired proportions with the construction logic of a futuristic armored assault vehicle.

Its primary silhouette is a broad forward wedge.

Key anatomical features:

- Four heavy load-bearing legs.
- Large protective chassis.
- Reinforced frontal horn or mechanical battering ram.
- Low center of gravity.
- Thick frontal armor.
- Compact sensor assembly.
- Integrated shield mechanisms.

### Combat Identity

Emphasizes frontal pressure, armored advances, powerful collisions, and close-range dominance.

The machine should feel difficult to displace but visibly slow to reposition.

### Design Challenges

- Distinguish frontal pressure from Siege Scarab's defensive posture.
- Prevent low height from granting unintended immunity to attacks.
- Make the horn and impact systems physically credible.

---

## 02 — Panther Wraith

**Original archetype:** Stealth Assassin  
**Role:** Fast, precise infiltration fighter  
**Attributes:** 8/8/2  
**Trait:** Camouflage  
**Power subtypes:** Cutting, Penetration  
**Concept status:** Retained

### Anatomy and Appearance

A narrow, athletic humanoid whose styling references feline predators without reproducing literal feline anatomy.

Key features:

- Long, articulated legs.
- Compact torso.
- Retractable cutting weapons.
- Segmented lightweight armor.
- Narrow sensor visor.
- Minimal projecting armor.
- Large ranges of joint movement.

### Combat Identity

Precision attacks, rapid repositioning, feints, and exploitation of openings.

Camouflage must preserve fair reaction opportunities.

### Design Challenges

- Distinguish its humanoid posture from Raptor Fang's animal-like locomotion.
- Prevent extremely low Resistance from making it unusable.
- Avoid excessively thin structural elements.

### Experimental Geometry

This character has been used in the G03–G06.2 geometry studies.

An illustrative height of **2.7 m** and horizontal mobility assumptions have been explored. These are not final model dimensions.

## 03 — Falcon Raptor

**Original archetype:** Aerial Bird  
**Role:** Agile aerial interceptor  
**Attributes:** 5/9/4  
**Trait:** Flight  
**Power subtypes:** Energy, Kinetic  
**Concept status:** Retained

### Anatomy and Appearance

A mechanical bird of prey with foldable wings, articulated talons, and a compact central body.

Key features:

- Triangular wing silhouette.
- Functional wing hinges.
- Retractable or compacted wings in ground combat.
- Claw-like manipulators.
- Reinforced forward striking structure.
- Integrated non-atmosphere-dependent propulsion.

### Combat Identity

Rapid aerial repositioning, controlled dives, claw attacks, and precise mobility.

### Design Challenges

- Maintain readable anatomy when the wings fold.
- Protect large exposed wing surfaces.
- Differentiate its elegant aerial movement from Pterosaur Striker's heavier assault style.
- Operate within universal flight restrictions.

## 04 — Siege Scarab

**Role:** Defensive area controller  
**Attributes:** 4/4/10  
**Traits:** Energy Shield, Counter  
**Power subtypes:** Impact, Electric  
**Concept status:** Refined

### Anatomy and Appearance

A heavily armored hexapod inspired by beetles and defensive industrial machinery.

Key features:

- Broad protective carapace.
- Six compact supporting legs.
- Low internal body placement.
- Retractable vulnerable structures.
- Powerful anchoring mechanisms.
- Concealed or protected offensive appendages.

### Combat Identity

Guarding, positional denial, counterattacks, and resisting heavy pressure.

### Design Challenges

- Avoid an overly static silhouette.

**Attributes:** 9/5/4  
**Traits:** None assigned

**Concept status:** Retained

A gorilla-proportioned industrial humanoid with extraordinarily large mechanical arms.

Key features:

## 06 — Manta Skimmer

**Replaces:** Disc Drone  
**Role:** Fast, low-profile hovering fighter  
**Inherited attributes:** 5/10/3 — provisional  
**Inherited traits:** Camouflage, Flight — provisional  
**Inherited power subtypes:** Electromagnetic, Electric — provisional  
**Concept status:** Replacement selected; mechanics not finalized

### Anatomy and Appearance

A hovering, manta-ray-inspired machine with an extremely broad and flattened profile.

Key features:

- Diamond-shaped primary silhouette.
- Wide articulated lateral fins.
- Protected central core.
- Ventral propulsion assemblies.
- Reinforced striking edges.
- Directional sensor clusters.
- Limited movable manipulators integrated into the body.

### Combat Identity

Low-altitude gliding, rotating strikes, rapid lateral movement, and unusual approach angles.

### Design Challenges

## 07 — Titan Atlas

**Original archetype:** Colossus  
**Role:** Monumental heavyweight  
**Attributes:** 10/2/6  
**Traits:** None assigned  
**Power subtypes:** Impact, Structural, Concussive  
**Concept status:** Retained

### Anatomy and Appearance

A colossal humanoid with the visual language of large industrial construction equipment.

Key features:

- Exceptional height.
- Thick structural limbs.
- Powerful load-bearing legs.
- Large manipulators.
- Massive central structural supports.
- Restrained decorative armor.
- Industrial mechanisms clearly supporting the machine's weight.

### Combat Identity

Enormous reach, powerful attacks, slower commitment, and strong positional pressure.

### Design Challenges

- Ensure smaller opponents can meaningfully engage it.
- Provide credible low-target attacks.
- Maintain fair attack startup and recovery.
- Prevent oversized pushboxes from breaking movement interactions.

### Experimental Geometry

Earlier prototype studies explored a **7.2 m height**, with approximately 2.7 m heavy-punch reach.

The attack geometry was adjusted to allow contact with much smaller opponents.

These are experimental dimensions, not approved production specifications.

## 17 — Monowheel Striker

**Original archetype:** New  
**Role:** High-mobility rolling combatant  
**Attributes:** Not assigned  
**Traits:** Not assigned  
**Power subtypes:** Not assigned  
**Concept status:** Single giant central wheel selected; detailed implementation provisional

### Anatomy and Appearance

A single massive wheel surrounds a suspended and armored central module.

Its dominant silhouette is nearly circular.

Key features:

- Large external wheel or segmented rolling ring.
- Protected central mechanical core.
- Internal bearing and suspension system.
- Retractable combat manipulators.
- Directional sensor cluster.
- Active balance control.
- Small deployable stabilizing supports.
- Possible corrective thrusters, subject to tournament compliance.

The wheel should dominate the machine's visible proportions, while the central module remains compact.

### Combat Identity

Rolling approaches, abrupt repositioning, momentum-based impacts, and attacks combining manipulators with the wheel's rotating structure.

Ordinary movement does not automatically inflict damage. Offensive collisions must correspond to valid attack actions.

### Proposed Component Mapping

| Universal slot | Physical implementation |
|---|---|
| Arms | Retractable manipulators |
| Legs | Wheel and drive assembly |
| Torso | Central chassis |
| Head | Directional sensor cluster |

### Design Challenges

- Maintain lateral stability under attack.
- Clearly indicate facing direction.
- Distinguish movement animation from offensive attacks.
- Make recovery from falls physically understandable.
- Preserve low attacks, aerial attacks, grabs, and anti-air functionality.
- Avoid making continuous rolling contact a persistent offensive advantage.

The wheel configuration is selected, but the proportions, colors, specific mechanisms, and move set remain unapproved.

The previous 16-mecha review divided the roster into three design-priority groups. Monowheel Striker was added afterward.

These are **art exploration priorities**, not final judgments of quality or release eligibility.

## Priority A — Core Visual References

- Rhino Bastion
- Panther Wraith
- Falcon Raptor
- Forge Ape
- Manta Skimmer
- Titan Atlas
- Viper Coil
- Tripod Core

## Priority B — Strong Candidates Needing Refinement

- Siege Scarab

These have strong recognizable shapes but require additional differentiation.

## Priority C — Experimental or Overlapping Designs

## Newly Added

It should receive early silhouette and engineering studies because its single-wheel anatomy presents unusually valuable design challenges.

| Monowheel / Manta | Ground rolling momentum versus low-altitude hovering |

The art team should test these contrasts in monochrome silhouette sheets before adding textures or surface details.

# 9. Geometry and Animation Status

Prior prototype work has focused on three mechas:

- Panther Wraith / Stealth Assassin
- Rhino Bastion / Heavy Tank
- Titan Atlas / Colossus

The geometry work includes:

- Two-dimensional fighting coordinates.
- Pushboxes, hurtboxes, hitboxes, and grab regions.
- Anatomical mapping to the four component slots.
- Basic dynamic attack trajectories.
- Exploratory attack startup, active, and recovery frames.
- Crouching and partial guard interactions.
- Basic proximity and movement checks.
- Simplified reduced-gravity descent calculations.

The G06.2 prototype reported passing its automated checks under its chosen abstractions. These checks do not demonstrate competitive balance or validate production-quality animation and collision models.

Exact mecha dimensions, hitbox shapes, and frame data remain provisional.

No full move library has yet been validated for all 17 mechas.

# 10. Standard Design Deliverables

For each mecha, the design team should eventually provide:

### A. Visual Identity

- Final candidate name.
- Silhouette sheet.
- Front, side, and three-quarter views.
- Height and mass estimates.
- Color palette.
- Materials and surface treatment.
- Readable sensor and weapon placements.

### B. Mechanical Anatomy

- Main structural frame.
- Propulsion and locomotion mechanisms.
- Joints and actuator placements.
- Stabilization systems.
- Armor and vulnerable areas.
- Four functional component mappings.
- Operational, Damaged, and Critical visual states.

### C. Combat Identity

- Final P/A/R attributes.
- Approved special traits.
- Approved power subtypes.
- Twelve normal attacks.
- Universal grab animation.
- Three special families.
- Attack range and recovery characteristics.
- Primary strengths, weaknesses, and counterplay.

### D. Tournament Compliance

- Earth, Mars, and Moon gravity compatibility.
- MUD compatibility.
- Flight and levitation restrictions.
- Projectile activation restrictions.
- No prohibited autonomous combat decisions.
- No prohibited detached drones or explosive projectiles.

### E. Originality and Rights

- Original development sketches.
- Reference and inspiration notes.
- Distinctive design rationale.
- Name-clearance review.
- Final approval record.

# 13. Document Governance

This guide consolidates the working roster and visual/anatomical direction established through November 17, 2026.

It supplements, rather than automatically overrides, the project's established combat mechanics and tournament regulations.

**Established decisions:** shared combat architecture, four component slots, five trait categories, twelve power subtypes, human piloting, tournament restrictions, the 17-candidate roster, the Manta and Pterosaur replacements, and Monowheel's single giant wheel.

**Working proposals:** individual attributes, inherited traits, visual palettes, detailed materials, movement styles, names, and most per-mecha attack concepts.

**Not yet validated:** competitive balance, real-world engineering feasibility, full mechanical collision behavior, and complete 17-mecha implementation.

The guiding principle for future revisions is:

**Distinctive futuristic machines, credible mechanical functions, expressive fighting styles, and one coherent competitive ruleset.**
